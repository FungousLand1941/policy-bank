01

Abstract

The 2026 BEHAVIOR Challenge asks one system to complete 100 long-horizon household tasks in simulation. Rather than train a single generalist, we assemble a bank of ten public and lightly fine-tuned vision-language-action checkpoints and route each task to one of them. Routing uses only development instances: a hierarchical empirical-Bayes model combines public reference results, policy-level estimates paired against a reference policy, and our ledger of 1,146 development rollouts, and a conservative rule keeps the incumbent policy unless a challenger also beats it on shared instances. The frozen route was evaluated once on the ten public reporting instances of every task. It reaches Q = 0.156 (95% CI 0.144–0.168) over 1,000 episodes, earning partial credit on 71 tasks but full success in only 37 episodes. Development estimates rank tasks well (Spearman 0.68) yet are optimistic by 0.05 on average, and rollout noise (s.d. 0.20 within a task) means telling apart two policies that differ by 0.05 takes about 250 rollouts each. We release the route, all 1,000 metric files, the development ledger, a reimplementation of the selector that reproduces 48 of 50 stage-2 decisions, and a fail-stop serving layer for hidden evaluation.

02

How the route is chosen

Each task is routed to exactly one policy. The choice uses development instances 311–320 only; the ten public reporting instances (301–310) were run once, after the route was frozen.

Prior for tasks 0–49

These tasks have public 2025 reference scores. A policy's prior mean is a transfer factor times its reference score. The factor is estimated from our own development rollouts on other tasks: for example 0.55 ± 0.09 for pt50 and 0.81 for the 2025 winner.

Prior for tasks 50–99

These tasks have no reference. The prior is policy-level: the reference policy's level plus the mean paired difference on shared tasks, shrunk toward the policy's family and widened by a between-task s.d. of 0.1.

Switching rules

The incumbent keeps a task unless a challenger clears every rule:

  • at least 2 of its own development rollouts on the task;
  • beats the incumbent on at least 2 shared instances;
  • posterior gain of at least 0.01;
  • no veto;
  • one checkpoint per family.

Two stages

Stage 1 routed – tasks early. The other – were deferred and routed in stage 2 from 860 development rollouts; the stage-2 route was frozen on 2026-10-13 at 12:54 UTC.

Serving for the hidden test

The organizers run the hidden test (instances 321–340) on their own machines. Our serving layer is fail-stop: it answers each query exactly as the published contract specifies, or it stops.

  • No retries. A failed query is not re-sent.
  • No substitute actions. The server never invents an action the policy did not produce.
  • No fallback checkpoint. Each task is served by its routed policy only.
  • No automatic restart. A stopped server stays stopped.
  • Malformed frames or non-finite actions end the session with close code 1011.
  • A second connection is refused with close code 1013.
08

Disclosures

  1. Simulator version. Public results were produced with Isaac Sim 6.0.1, because Isaac Sim 5.1 was incompatible with the cluster's driver. The organizers allowed Isaac 6 for public results; hidden evaluation uses 5.1.
  2. What Q = 0.156 is. It is a self-evaluated score on the public instances. It is not a hidden-test score and not a placement.
  3. Duplicate cells. Two cells completed twice. In both, the first completed attempt was kept regardless of score: spraying for bugs, instance 307 (kept 0.5, excluded 0.0), and cook brussels sprouts, instance 303 (kept 0.0, excluded 0.0).
  4. Replay settings. Of the – episodes, – ran with replay 32 and – with replay 20. The other – ran with replay 0 (one model query per step) in a recovery pass.
  5. Scorer denominator. The organizer scorer copy used a 20-instance denominator, which gives 0.078; the published contract uses 10. No file was edited.
  6. Weights and licences. Model weights are not redistributed here. Redistribution terms for the Comet-derived and amyjia checkpoints are unresolved.
  7. Qualification status. Synthetic-input GPU startup tests passed on an RTX 3090 for all ten models. No full end-to-end hidden-protocol qualification (Isaac 5.1, 1 FPS, Turing GPU) has been completed.
